WHY IS IT SO HARD FOR YOUNG PEOPLE
TO GET HIRED IN UNITED KINGDOM?

01

Credential inflation

As more people earn degrees, employers have quietly raised entry requirements without job complexity actually rising, turning the degree into a screening filter rather than a genuine skill signal.

02

Vanishing entry-level postings

Starter-job vacancies have fallen to some of their lowest levels in over a decade outside the pandemic, shrinking the number of first rungs on the career ladder.

03

The rising cost of a first hire

Higher employer payroll taxes and minimum wage increases have made junior hires more expensive right as employer demand has softened.

04

Long-term scarring

With over a fifth of young jobseekers out of work for a year or more, extended joblessness itself becomes a barrier, as employers grow wary of lengthening employment gaps.

Our take

A degree in the UK increasingly proves you can study — not that you can do the job. We close that gap by letting young people complete real paid tasks and build a verified skills record from day one, giving employers a faster, more trustworthy signal than credentials alone.

Sources: Office for National Statistics (ONS), Labour Market Statistics, July 2026 · Institute for the Future of Work (IFOW) · Work Foundation, Lancaster University.
